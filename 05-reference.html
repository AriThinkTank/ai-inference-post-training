E.01

Four sources that cover nearly all of this properly

R.01

Nathan Lambert, Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback

Free at rlhfbook.com. Also arXiv:2504.12501. Print edition from Manning, 2026.

The best single source for chapter 02, and it isn't close. Chapters 4 to 7 cover instruction tuning, reward models, policy gradients and reasoning. Lambert runs post-training at the Allen Institute, so it reads like someone describing their own job rather than surveying a field.

rlhfbook.com ↗

R.02

Jacob Austin and others, How To Scale Your Model

Free at jax-ml.github.io/scaling-book. Google DeepMind, 2025.

The best single source for chapter 03. Chapter 7 gives you the arithmetic of prefill, decode and the KV cache. Chapter 8 works through the cost of serving a real model. It assumes algebra and nothing more, which is rarer than it should be.

jax-ml.github.io/scaling-book ↗

R.03

Epoch AI, Trends dashboard and Gradient Updates

Free at epoch.ai/trends and epochai.substack.com. Updated continuously.

The best source for chapter 04. Independent, publishes its method and its data, and the first place to check any claim about where AI compute goes or what it costs. If a number in a news story disagrees with Epoch, believe Epoch until shown otherwise.

epoch.ai/trends ↗

R.04

Georgia Tech CS 7643, lecture 23

Slides, Spring 2026, at faculty.cc.gatech.edu.

Supervised fine-tuning through to GRPO in one sitting, equations included. Useful if you want the maths in compact form, and short enough to read on a flight.

In closing

Pretraining built the capability.
Post-training made it usable.
Inference decides who can afford it.
