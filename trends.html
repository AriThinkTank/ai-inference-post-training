How the Chokepoints Have Moved

Two of these lines are running in opposite directions at extraordinary speed, and the gap between them is the single most important fact in AI economics.

Five series

Pick one and read the growth rate

The scissors

Making the frontier costs more every year. Reaching last year’s frontier costs less.

These two trends are the whole strategic picture, and they point in opposite directions.

Going up

Frontier training cost, about doubling a year

$4.6m for GPT-3 in 2020. $79m for GPT-4 in 2023. $191m for Gemini Ultra in 2024. A range of $200m to $500m for the 2026 class. That is a compound rate above 100% a year, and it puts the frontier further out of reach for every country every year.

Coming down

Price of a fixed capability, about 90% a year

GPT-3 quality cost $60 per million tokens in 2021 and $0.06 by 2024. A thousandfold fall in three years. Epoch AI measures a median decline of 50 times a year across capability milestones, rising to about 200 times a year for models released since January 2024.

What the scissors mean for a country like IndiaChasing the frontier gets harder every year and is a race India will not win on capital. Deploying last year’s frontier gets dramatically cheaper every year, and is a race India can win on scale, languages and cost. The two curves are not a dilemma. They are an instruction.
Frontier costs: Stanford AI Index with Epoch AI for 2023 and 2024, Epoch AI cost model elsewhere. Price decline: a16z LLMflation and Epoch AI, LLM Inference Price Trends. The decline ranges from 9 to 900 times a year depending on the capability milestone, so the median is a summary of a very wide distribution.
Where the decline comes from

Three forces, and only one of them can be restricted

Force 1

Algorithmic efficiency, about 3× a year

Better methods for the same result. Estimated at roughly three times a year by Ho and colleagues via Epoch AI, and independently at about three times a year by Gundlach and colleagues after stripping out competition and hardware effects.

Force 2

Hardware price-performance, about 1.3× a year

More computation per dollar of silicon. The slowest of the three, and the only one an export control reaches.

Force 3

Competition

Providers undercutting each other. Large, volatile, and the reason headline price declines look faster than underlying efficiency gains.

The asymmetry worth naming

Algorithmic efficiency travels through published papers and open weights, and arrives in India at the same time as everywhere else. Hardware price-performance arrives through purchase, and can be slowed. So an export control slows the 1.3× and leaves the 3× untouched, which is why the delay it buys is measured in a small number of years rather than in decades. The denial half-life calculator on the open weights page puts a number on it.

India’s own lines

Two Indian series, and one that needs a discontinuity

Achieved, 2020 to 2026
20.6%

Compound annual growth in Indian colocation data centre capacity. A real and creditable rate.

Required, 2026 to 2029
55.4%

Growth needed to reach the 6 GW projection. That is 2.7 times the rate India has managed for six years, sustained for three more.

How to read that gap

It does not make the projection wrong. Capital commitments have genuinely stepped up and 2025 added a record amount of capacity. It does mean the projection assumes a discontinuity rather than a trend, and anyone planning against it should say which discontinuity they are relying on.

With four observations a growth rate can be fitted but inference cannot be done, and no confidence interval is quoted here because none would mean anything.
