Where the Money Goes

A frontier pretraining run and a serious post-training programme differ by a factor of about forty in cost. They also differ completely in what that money buys, and that second difference is the one with strategic consequences.

Before relying on any of the figures below, it is worth knowing how well they stand up. We tested all three against independent sources and one of them did not survive as written. That work is on the workings page, and it is probably the more useful read.

Reading the stacks

What the three shapes tell you

Pretraining

A hardware business

Somewhere between 47 and 65 per cent of the bill is accelerators, servers and interconnect. The range is that wide because the underlying cost model puts research staff anywhere from 29 to 49 per cent, and hardware takes the remainder. We use 62 here, which sits near the favourable end. This is the stage that looks like semiconductor policy, because that is largely what it is.

Post-training

A labour business, up to a point

70 per cent of the bill is human data and graded environments, and compute is a fifth. That holds for a light recipe of the sort India would realistically run. Push reinforcement learning to frontier intensity and compute overtakes everything else, which we calculate. Below that crossing point it is priced like professional services.

Inference

A property business

Chips still lead at about half, but power, buildings and staff come to between 39 and 47 per cent depending on whose model you use. Four independent estimates land within eight points of each other, which makes this the firmest of the three splits. Anyone who has financed a data centre will recognise the shape.

The line that surprises people

Reinforcement environments cost real money

A single interface turned into a trainable environment runs to about $20,000. A complex application rebuilt as one runs to about $300,000. Individual expert-written tasks go for $200 to $2,000 each, and exclusive rights cost several times the non-exclusive rate.

These are software engineering budgets, not data budgets. They are also entirely absent from Indian AI spending, and they are a quarter of what a serious post-training programme costs.

What an environment is, in one sentenceA test that a computer can mark on its own, so a model can be trained against it millions of times without a human in the loop.
Why they never appear in trade data

They are not sold across borders. Frontier laboratories build them in house and keep them there. Nothing to export means nothing to control, and also nothing to buy.

04 So

At this stage the binding constraint is finding credentialed people who will do careful work, not finding accelerators. That is a labour market problem, and India is unusually well placed in labour markets.
