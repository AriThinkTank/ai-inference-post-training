After the Model Is Trained

Most explanations of large language models stop at the end of pretraining: assemble a very large corpus, predict the next token, scale up. Almost everything that has changed in the past two years happens after that point. This is that part, drawn out in panels, for a reader who has never opened a machine-learning textbook and has no intention of starting.

Orientation

What this is for, and what it is not

The central point, in one breath

Capability is no longer mostly a property of the pretraining run. It is increasingly a property of what is done to the weights afterwards, and of how much compute is spent at the moment a question is answered.

That shift is what drives almost every policy question in chapter 04. If you read nothing else, read that sentence twice.

A second model, trained to guess what a human would have preferred. Confident, cheap, and fooled more easily than anyone would like.

Vera · the checker

Not a model at all. A program that runs the code, or compares the answer, and says pass or fail. Cannot be flattered.

The map · one screen

From raw text to an answer on a screen, in seven stages

The first five change the weights. The last two spend compute at the moment of use. Almost every confusion in public discussion comes from treating all seven as one thing called “training”.

The loop that makes this hard to separate

Stages P.03 to P.05 run the model in order to generate their own training data. Training now consumes very large amounts of inference, so the two halves of the map are no longer independent activities with independent costs. When somebody quotes a training figure and an inference figure, ask which column the rollouts were counted in. Often, neither.

True in 2022

Almost all capability came from pretraining. Post-training was a thin supervised layer.

True in 2026

Preference training, verifiable rewards and agentic reinforcement learning carry a large and rising share of compute and capability.

Still contested

Whether these stages create capability, or only make capability that was already there reliable.
