After the Model Is Trained

A playbook in panels

Most explanations of AI stop the moment pretraining ends. Read the internet, predict the next word, scale up, the end.

That's roughly half the story, and lately the less interesting half. This is the rest of it.

No maths beyond arithmetic. Over thirty things to click. Every number says where it came from, and when the source is a company marking its own homework, it says that too.

The cast

Four characters, doing four real jobs

They're inventions. What they do isn't. Each one stands in for something that actually exists in the pipeline.

Ira

Ten years old

Asks the obvious question that everyone else is too embarrassed to ask. Usually the right one.

Echo

The model

Does exactly what it was trained to do, which is rarely what you wanted. Never lies about it either.

Mark

The reward model

Marks answers on a human's behalf. Means well. Falls for long answers, confident tone and tidy headings.

Vera

The verifier

A program. Runs the code, checks the proof, says pass or fail. Cannot be flattered. Also can't mark an essay.

The map

Seven stages, and only five of them touch the model

People say "training" as though it were one event. It's seven, and the last two happen every time you press enter.

Wait, it loops?Yes. Stages 2 to 5 run again and again, often weekly. A frontier model isn't released once. It's more like a newspaper than a bridge.
2022

Almost all the compute went into stage 1. Post-training was a small polish at the end.

2026

Stages 2 to 5 take a large and growing share, and stage 7 didn't meaningfully exist three years ago.

Still argued about

Whether the later stages create new ability or just uncover what stage 1 already put there. Genuinely unsettled.

Stage naming follows Lambert, arXiv:2504.12501, ch. 3; serving stages follow Austin et al., How To Scale Your Model, ch. 7.
