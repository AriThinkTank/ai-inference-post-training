Chapter 04 · Why it matters for policy

The Instruments Were Built for a Different Picture

The technical material in the first three chapters has a small number of consequences for how AI capability is measured, controlled and governed. Each is stated below as a conditional claim, with the evidence for it graded. None of them is a recommendation.

D.01 · The numbers, and why they disagree

Inference has become the larger share of compute, on most accounts

Published estimates of the split differ widely, mostly over what is being counted rather than over the trend.

Estimate 1 · one firm, 2024
~30%

About 30 per cent of one developer’s compute spending went on inference. Epoch AI’s estimate. The denominator is one company’s own spending, in a year before reasoning models were widely deployed.

Estimate 2 · industry, 2026
~2/3

About two-thirds of all AI compute now goes on inference. A consultancy forecast reported second-hand, with a stated path from a third in 2023 through a half in 2025.

Estimate 3 · the robust one
54–62%

Share of operating costs taken by compute at the firms Epoch can model. Research and inference compute together, and the figure most robust to the counting problem below.

Why the first two figures are not comparable

01 · Different denominators

One firm’s spending against the world’s installed capacity. Frontier developers are estimated to use under half of global AI compute.

02 · A boundary problem

The rollouts reinforcement learning consumes are inference runs, and are sometimes counted in neither column.

03 · Different source quality

Epoch publishes its method and data. The industry-wide figure is a consultancy forecast reported second-hand.

What can safely be said

Inference is a large and rising share of AI compute. Any percentage needs its source and its denominator attached, and a figure quoted without both should not be load-bearing in an argument.

Epoch AI, Trends dashboard and Data Insights, February 2026 (epoch.ai/trends); Epoch AI, ‘Frontier labs don’t use most AI compute (yet)’, May 2026; Deloitte 2026 TMT Predictions as reported in trade coverage, not independently verified.

What the technical picture does to compute-based controls

01
The picture the instruments were designed around

Capability is created once, in a very large training run, on identifiable hardware, by a small number of firms.

The narrow claim

Each element of the original picture is now weaker than it was. That is not the same as saying the instruments do not work.

The fair counter-argument

None of this shows that compute controls do not work

Very large pretraining runs remain difficult to hide and impossible to do without advanced accelerators, and hardware is still the most observable input in the whole system. Nothing in the first three chapters changes that.

The narrower claim is that the share of capability those controls reach appears to be falling, and that anything measured only at training time now misses more than it once did. Those are different claims and should not be run together.

D.03 · Implication

Capability diffuses on a much shorter clock than it is created

Three mechanisms from chapter 02, taken together, compress the gap between a frontier release and widely available capability of a similar kind.

Mechanism 1

Distillation

A frontier model’s outputs, including its reasoning traces, train much smaller models that reproduce a good deal of its behaviour on the tasks distilled.

Mechanism 2

Published methods

The techniques in chapter 02 are documented in the open literature within months. Sequence and data quality are the hard parts, not the algorithms.

Mechanism 3

Quantisation

Once a capable open model exists, compression moves it onto progressively cheaper hardware, and eventually onto machines no control regime observes.

What follows for a country in India’s position

The binding constraint may not be access to weights

The practical question is less about access to the largest models and more about whether the complements are in place: accelerators to run inference at national scale, engineering capacity at layer 4 of the serving stack, and verifiable-reward environments for domains that matter locally.

On the present evidence the binding constraints appear to be serving capacity and evaluation capability rather than access to weights. That judgement is an inference from the technical picture, not a measured finding, and it is offered as such.

The honest qualification

Diffusion is uneven, and shallower than headlines suggest. A distilled model matches its teacher on the distribution it was distilled for and degrades away from it.

Agentic capability, which depends on maintained environments rather than on weights, has diffused much more slowly than reasoning ability. Anyone arguing that everything diffuses in weeks should be asked about environments.

D.04 · Implication

Evaluating a model no longer means evaluating a fixed thing

The same weights, deployed with different answer-time budgets, behave like different systems. This complicates every regime that attaches obligations to a model rather than to a deployment.

Configuration A

Minimum budget

Capability 

No extended thinking, one sample, no tools.

Configuration B

Extended thinking

Capability 

A long reasoning chain before answering.

Configuration C

Thinking, sampling and tools

Capability 

Many attempts, a verifier, a search loop, retries.

Same weights, different deployed capability. Bar lengths are illustrative and are not measurements. Try the dial on chapter 03.
For evaluation

A benchmark result is a property of a model and a budget. Results reported without the budget are not comparable, and the budget is rarely disclosed.

For disclosure

A safety evaluation performed at one budget says little about behaviour at ten times that budget, which a deployer may simply choose to allow.

For thresholds

Any rule defined over training compute leaves answer-time compute unbounded. The two are substitutable to a degree that is not yet well characterised.

A constructive reading

This also suggests where useful disclosure could sit

On the deployment configuration rather than on the weights. Reporting the answer-time budget alongside an evaluation result would cost a developer very little and would make published figures comparable with one another. That is offered as an option rather than as a recommendation.

Answer-time scaling behaviour: Lambert, arXiv:2504.12501, ch. 7. The disclosure suggestion is the author’s.
D.05 · Limitations

What is not settled, stated plainly

The material in chapters 02 and 03 is a mixture of published research, developer disclosure and vendor engineering claims of varying quality. These are the points where this site is least secure.

U.01 Whether reinforcement learning creates capability or reveals it Contested in the 2026 literature. If it mostly elicits capability already present, the case for controlling pretraining compute is stronger than this site implies.
U.02 How far answer-time compute substitutes for training compute Both are known to help. The exchange rate between them is not well characterised, and it very likely differs by task type.
U.03 The true training and inference compute split No reliable public series exists. The estimates on this page differ by a factor of two and are not measuring the same quantity.
U.04 Serving performance figures Most 2026 numbers on batching, caching and speculation come from the vendors whose systems they describe, and have not been independently reproduced.
How the sources were graded

Three tiers, applied throughout

Peer-reviewed papers and primary technical reports are treated as reliable for mechanism. Developer blogs are treated as reliable for what that developer did, and unreliable for comparison. Consultancy forecasts and vendor benchmarks are reported with the source named, and are not relied on for any load-bearing claim.

On the frameworks

Organising devices, not findings

The supervision taxonomy, the verifiability gradient and the six-layer stack were assembled for the underlying deck. They are organising devices, and no claim to novelty is made for them.

Contested status of elicitation versus capability: arXiv:2602.01599 and related 2026 work. Compute-split estimates: Epoch AI, February 2026, against Deloitte 2026 TMT Predictions.
In closing

Three habits, which are the part worth keeping

01

When a model release is announced

Ask which measures it was trained against. That predicts both its strengths and its disappointments, and it is usually answerable from the release notes.

02

When a benchmark figure is quoted

Ask at what answer-time budget it was obtained. A number without a budget is not comparable to another number without a budget.

03

When a compute figure is quoted

Ask what was counted and what was left out. The rollouts that reinforcement learning consumes are the usual missing column.

Corrections and disagreements are welcome, particularly on the four points listed under D.05.
