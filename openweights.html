Overview / Open weights

04

What Open Weights Actually Relieve

Open weights are talked about as though they either solve everything or nothing. They do neither. They relieve some chokepoints almost completely, leave others untouched, and do nothing at all about the two lines that post-training actually runs on.

The measurement

Four months behind, and holding

Epoch AI tracks open and closed capability on a composite index. Since January 2026 the best open-weight models have trailed the best closed models by an average of four months, or eight index points with a 90% confidence interval of 7 to 11. Their earlier analysis, covering January 2023 to October 2025, put it at three months.

The gap today
4 months

Roughly one minor version. Epoch compares it to the distance between GPT-5 and GPT-5.5.

In index points
8 ECI

90% confidence interval 7 to 11. Requiring strict dominance rather than a statistical tie pushes it to about six months.

Direction of travel
Widening

Three months to four. Small, but not the closing gap the open-weight case usually assumes.

Who publishes them
China

Chinese laboratories now lead open-weight rankings. Epoch puts Chinese models about 7 months behind the US frontier since 2023.

Epoch AI, Open models lag state-of-the-art closed models by 4 months, 29 May 2026, and the October 2025 Data Insight. Epoch note two honest caveats: open models may overfit public benchmarks, and closed laboratories withhold their best systems from public comparison, so the true lag may be larger.
The bit that should worry an Indian plannerMost usable open weights now come from Chinese laboratories, which themselves trail the US frontier by about seven months. A country relying on open weights inherits both lags rather than one, and it inherits a dependency on the release decisions of a third country it has no leverage over.

The big relief

Open weights make the pretraining problem go away by making it unnecessary

Start from published weights and you skip the one job that needs thousands of accelerators wired together as a single machine. The interconnect line, which is 11% of a pretraining budget and the hardest thing to buy, drops out entirely. So does the need for a contiguous cluster, which is India's actual constraint rather than the headline GPU count.

This is not a small point. It converts India's hardest hardware problem into somebody else's solved problem.

The non-relief

And they do nothing about the two lines that carry 70% of the post-training bill

Published weights arrive without the data that shaped them and without the environments they were trained against. That is not an oversight. Those are the assets, and no laboratory publishes them.

Which is why a released open model is hard to push much further. You have the artefact and none of the tooling that made it, and buying the tooling is the expensive part.

The equation

How many years does an export control actually buy?

Controls are usually discussed as though they either work or fail. A better question is how long they delay somebody, and that is answerable. Two rates do the work: algorithmic efficiency improves at about three times a year, and hardware price-performance at about 1.3 times a year. Both reach a restricted country anyway, through published research and open weights.

Equation E5, derived from E4

T = ln(R) ÷ ln(α · h)

T is years of delay. R is how many times short of the frontier a country's accessible effective compute is. α is algorithmic efficiency gain per year, h is hardware price-performance gain per year. It follows from writing effective compute as Ceff(t) = Cphys × αt and asking when the restricted party reaches today's frontier requirement.

What this does and does not show

It shows when compute stops being the binding constraint. It says nothing about whether the people, the data and the environments are in place to use that compute. On the evidence elsewhere on this dashboard, for India those bind first, which means the delay an export control buys against India is shorter than the delay India imposes on itself by not funding the other half of the bill.

What follows

Three things an Indian policymaker could do with this

O.01 Mirror significant open releases as stated national policy A licence binds future releases. Weights already downloaded cannot be recalled by anyone, in any jurisdiction. Maintaining national mirrors costs a rounding error and removes an entire class of disruption. Cost: accepting a four-month capability lag, and a dependency on Chinese release decisions.
O.02 Spend on the half open weights do not supply If pretraining is solved by somebody else's publication, the marginal rupee should go to data and environments, which nobody publishes and which are 70% of the post-training bill. This is the clearest spending implication on the whole dashboard. Cost: unglamorous, with no groundbreaking to announce.
O.03 Treat the programming stack as the software chokepoint, not the framework PyTorch is 63% concentrated and permissively licensed, so it is close to harmless. CUDA is 90% concentrated, proprietary and revocable, and open weights do nothing about it. Any Indian sovereignty effort in AI software that targets frameworks rather than the accelerator programming layer is aiming at the wrong thing. Cost: the alternative stacks are genuinely less mature.

Pretraining buys hardware.
Post-training buys people.
Inference buys buildings and power.
