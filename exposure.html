Who Holds Each Line of the Bill

Counting suppliers is only half the question. What matters is how much of a stage's budget sits with suppliers who can be told to stop, and whether anything else would do the job.

The suppliers

Four places where the world has almost no second source

These four sit inside the accelerator line, which is 33% of a pretraining budget and 50% of an inference budget.

Lithography machines
1 firm

ASML makes every advanced one. Dutch licensing since 2019.

Leading-edge foundry
~90%

TSMC's share of the most advanced output. Samsung and Intel share the rest.

Stacked memory
3 firms

SK hynix 58%, Samsung 21%, Micron 21%. Controlled since December 2024.

AI accelerators
~88%

NVIDIA's share of the merchant market. AMD holds most of the remainder.

Company filings and Counterpoint Research quarterly share data, Q1 2026. All four are graded A or B in the data file.

And one place where concentration means nothing

The software almost every model is built with has roughly 63% share, held by one project. That sounds like a chokepoint and is not one, because it is open source: if the terms ever changed, the code is already on every machine that uses it and can be copied and carried on.

The programming layer directly beneath it has 90% share and is proprietary. That one is a chokepoint. Same market structure, opposite strategic meaning, and the difference is licensing rather than share.

The test to applyNot "how many suppliers are there" but "if this supplier were told to stop selling to India tomorrow, what would break, and for how long". Those two questions have different answers far more often than supply chain reports suggest.
The instruments

What each export control actually reaches

Every instrument in force today points at the same line of the bill: the chips. None of them reaches the human data, the environments, the electricity, the buildings or the staff.

India is not blocked. India is exposed to changes of mind.

No instrument currently embargoes India. Licences are available and chips arrive. The AI Diffusion Framework placed India in a middle tier in January 2025 and was withdrawn in May, with nothing about India having changed in the interval.

The rules moved four times in eighteen months, in both directions. That is the actual problem. A data centre is a seven-year capital commitment and it cannot be planned against an eighteen-month rule cycle.

The instrument to watch

Location attestation in the chip

The Chip Security Act passed committee in March 2026 and is not law. If it becomes law it would turn a one-time export decision into a standing dependency, because the chip keeps reporting where it is for its whole working life.

The instrument nobody needs to write

Design software is cloud-delivered and metered by licence. Cutting it off requires no shipment to be stopped and no port to be involved. It works within one billing cycle.

Putting the two together

Cost-weighted exposure, by stage

Take each line of the bill, multiply its share of the budget by how controllable it is, then discount it by how easily something else would do. Sum across the stage. The result ranks the three stages against each other.

Pretraining, 36

Most exposed, and by a distance. The chips are a third of the bill, there is no substitute for top-bin parts, and the interconnect comes bundled with them.

Inference, 22

Middling. Chips are half the bill, but older and cheaper parts genuinely work here, and the other half is power and property.

Post-training, 8

Barely exposed at all. Four-fifths of the budget buys human judgement, and no country has found a way to license that.

The awkward implicationThe stage most exposed to export controls is the one India was least likely to compete in anyway. The stage India could most plausibly lead is the one no control instrument touches. Which means the binding constraint on Indian AI is not export policy at all. It is whether anybody chooses to fund the unglamorous half of the bill.

Pretraining buys hardware.
Post-training buys people.
Inference buys buildings and power.
