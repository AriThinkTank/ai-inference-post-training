How Every Number Was Worked Out

Source grades

What the letters mean

A — primary: government release, company filing, or a technical report that states its method.
B — reputable secondary with a stated method.
C — industry estimate, method not published.
D — widely repeated, primary source not traceable. Used for context only and never in a calculation.

The calculations

Line by line

The pretraining cost split
Taken directly from Cottier et al., arXiv:2405.21015, the most thorough public cost model of frontier training. That paper puts server components at 15-22% of total, cluster interconnect at 9-13%, energy at 2-6%, and states that accelerator chips and staff costs each run to tens of millions. We take the midpoints: accelerators 33%, staff 30%, servers 18%, interconnect 11%, energy 4%, data 4%. The residual to 100% is absorbed into the data line, which is the weakest of the six.
The post-training cost split
Our own build, and the least firm thing on this dashboard. Compute is anchored to a disclosed figure: DeepSeek-R1's reinforcement learning stage was 147,000 GPU-hours, about $294,000 at $2 per GPU-hour. Human data is anchored to vendor pricing surveys giving $0.50-8 per generalist comparison and $20-100 and above per expert comparison, against the InstructGPT recipe of roughly 100,000 comparisons. Environments are anchored to reported prices of about $20,000 for a single interface environment and about $300,000 for a complex application clone. The resulting split is 45% data, 25% environments, 20% compute, 10% staff. Different volume assumptions move these materially, and anyone reworking them should say which volumes they used.
The inference cost split
Built from data centre economics rather than model economics, because that is what inference is. Accelerators amortised over a working life dominate at 50%; energy at 18% and facility at 17% follow from typical data centre cost structures at Indian power prices; networking and storage at 7% and engineering staff at 8% make up the remainder. Graded B and C. The lifetime inference bill for any specific model is not public anywhere, which is why stage-costs.csv carries a zero and a grade D against that row rather than an invented figure.
Control leverage, 0 to 1
Our own scale, and the most contestable thing here. 0 means no instrument exists and none is plausible, as with electricity or staff. 0.2 means competitive supply with a theoretical instrument. 0.6 means an instrument exists but supply is partly substitutable. 0.8 means an instrument exists and has been used. 1.0 means an instrument exists, has been used, and takes effect without any shipment being stopped. The basis for each score sits in the CSV so it can be rescored.
Substitutability, 0 to 1
How far something cheaper or unrestricted does the same job. Pretraining accelerators score 0.10 because older parts genuinely cannot substitute: memory bandwidth and interconnect are the point. Inference accelerators score 0.60 because older generations, lower-spec parts and compressed models all work. That single difference is what separates the two stages in the exposure index, and it is the assumption to attack first if you disagree with the conclusion.
Cost-weighted exposure
For each stage: sum of (share of budget × control leverage × (1 − substitutability)), expressed on a 0-100 scale. On the default weights this gives pretraining 36, inference 22, post-training 8. It is an ordinal comparison between stages. It is not a probability, not a risk score, and should never be quoted as either.
The fleet arithmetic
38,000 accelerators (MeitY and IndiaAI, December 2025) × 24 hours = 912,000 GPU-hours a day. DeepSeek-R1's 147,000 GPU-hours divided by that is 0.16 days, or 3.9 hours. DeepSeek-V3's 2,788,000 GPU-hours is 3.06 days. Both DeepSeek figures are as disclosed in the respective technical reports. Almost no other laboratory discloses them, so one developer's numbers should not be generalised.
Power and rack density
700 W per accelerator (H100 SXM datasheet). 38,000 accelerators is 27 MW of IT load, about 40 MW with cooling at a power usage effectiveness of 1.5. National colocation IT load is 1,600 MW (JLL, June 2026), so the fleet is 1.7% of it. Rack counts follow from dividing hall design density by 0.7 kW per accelerator. The modern reference point is a rack of 72 accelerators drawing about 120 kW.
Company margins, and why they are the firmest data here
NVIDIA fiscal 2026 from the Form 10-K filed with the SEC: revenue $215.9bn, GAAP gross margin 71.1 per cent, operating income $130.4bn giving an operating margin of 60.4. TSMC second quarter 2026 from investor relations: gross 67.7, operating 60.3, net 55.6. SK hynix second quarter 2026: operating profit KRW 60.54tn on revenue KRW 79.32tn, an operating margin of 76 per cent. All graded A because they are audited or company-published.

Packaging, assembly, data centre operation and annotation margins are industry estimates graded C, because those sectors are fragmented or privately held and do not publish comparable figures. Gross and operating margin are different measures and the table says which each row is; they should not be read across without noticing that.
Time and capital to build a domestic alternative
Our own estimates, and the softest numbers on this dashboard. They establish orders of magnitude and relative ordering rather than costings. Capital figures are indicative programme cost to reach a credible domestic capability, not to match world-leading scale. Where a timeline is set by equipment India cannot import rather than by money, the entry says so, because in those cases no budget decision changes the answer.
How the three headline claims were tested
Set out in full on the workings page, which is the right place to start if you intend to cite anything from here. In short: the inference split was checked against four independent estimates and survived, landing inside a 53 to 61 per cent range. The pretraining split survived but our figure sits near the favourable end of the published range rather than in the middle, so we now quote a range. The post-training claim did not survive as written, because reinforcement learning compute has scaled far beyond the DeepSeek disclosure it was anchored to; it has been replaced with a scale-dependent version and a calculated crossing point.
The labour-to-compute crossover in post-training
Compute cost = RL intensity × 2,788,000 pretraining GPU-hours × $2 per GPU-hour, both figures from DeepSeek's own V3 and R1 reports. Non-compute cost = comparisons × price each, plus interface environments at $20,000 and application clones at $300,000, plus a research team at about $1m. At the default inputs the crossing point is 50.2 per cent of pretraining compute; with expert-rate comparisons it moves to 148.9 per cent. Disclosed reference points: DeepSeek-R1-Zero at 3.75 per cent, DeepSeek-R1 at 5.3, Cursor Composer 1.5 above 100.
Sensitivity: which input carries the conclusion
A one-at-a-time test on the inference exposure index. Taking each input to both ends of its plausible range while holding the rest at default, the index moves furthest on accelerator substitutability, which swings it between about 10 and 35 against a default of 22. Every measured input moves it by five points or less. The uncomfortable implication, stated on the workings page rather than hidden here, is that the most influential number in this analysis is a judgement we assigned rather than a figure any source published.
Equations used, and where they come from
Full list with citations in equations.csv. The load-bearing ones: E1 training compute C = 6ND (Hoffmann et al., Chinchilla, arXiv:2203.15556; Kaplan et al., arXiv:2001.08361). E2 Herfindahl-Hirschman Index, the sum of squared percentage shares (US DOJ and FTC Merger Guidelines 2023). E4 effective compute Ceff(t) = Cphys × αt, with α the algorithmic efficiency multiplier. E6 KV cache memory per token M = 2 × L × Hkv × dhead × b (Kwon et al., arXiv:2309.06180). E8 compound annual growth from endpoints. E3 and E5 are our own constructions and are marked as such.
The denial half-life, equation E5
T = ln(R) ÷ ln(αh). Derived by setting Cphys(αh)T = Creq and solving for T, where R = Creq / Cphys. Parameter values: α = 3.0 per year, the algorithmic efficiency estimate of Ho et al. 2024 via Epoch AI, corroborated independently at about 3× per year by Gundlach et al., arXiv:2511.23455, who isolate algorithmic progress by controlling for competition and dividing out hardware price declines. h = 1.3 per year, Epoch AI's hardware price-performance estimate. Combined rate 3.9× per year. Worked example: a country held 30 times short of the frontier reaches today's frontier requirement in ln(30) / ln(3.9) = 2.50 years. The assumption to attack: that efficiency gains reach the restricted party. Open publication and open weights largely ensure this today, but a regime that also restricted publication would break the equation.
The open-weight relief scores
Control leverage on the same 0-1 scale, scored with and without a usable open-weight starting point. Our judgement, with the reasoning shown per line in the widget. The capability cost of that relief is measured rather than judged: Epoch AI's Capabilities Index puts the open-closed gap at 4 months and 8 ECI points, 90% confidence interval 7 to 11, as of 29 May 2026, widened from 3 months in their October 2025 analysis. Epoch note that open models may overfit public benchmarks and that closed laboratories withhold their best systems, so the true lag is probably larger rather than smaller.
Trend series and growth rates
All in trends.csv, computed with E8 from first and last observation. Frontier training cost: +105.9% a year across 2020-2026. Price of a fixed capability: −90.0% a year across 2021-2024, a thousandfold fall. Indian data centre capacity: +20.6% a year across 2020-2026 against the +55.4% required to reach the 2029 projection. With three to five observations these are fitted rates and nothing more. No regression is run and no confidence interval is quoted, because with this many points neither would mean anything.
Where the price decline comes from
Epoch AI measure the price of a fixed capability milestone falling between 9 and 900 times a year depending on the milestone, median about 50 times, rising to about 200 times for models released since January 2024. That headline combines three forces: algorithmic efficiency at about 3× a year, hardware price-performance at about 1.3× a year, and competition, which is large and volatile. Only the second of the three can be reached by an export control, which is the asymmetry that drives E5.
The scenario engine
Multiplicative, with every multiplier printed alongside the output. Build 0.9 to 2.63× (2.63 is the stated 100,000 target over 38,000), chip access 1.15 to 0.35×, housing fraction 0.20 to 1.0. Data and environment readiness is scored but deliberately does not enter the hardware arithmetic, because it cannot be bought with accelerators. The multipliers span a plausible range and are not estimated from anything.
Honesty section

What we could not do

Firm-level Indian GPU deployment Not published. Installed against empanelled counts cannot be verified beyond mid-2025.
Lifetime inference cost for any named model No developer discloses it. The 80-90% of lifetime compute figure is widely cited and traces to secondary sources only.
The 20% design workforce claim Could not be traced to any primary source. Graded D and used in no calculation here.
Environment pricing from a primary source Consistent across industry reporting but no vendor price list is public. Graded C, and it carries 25% of the post-training split.
Where this would change most

The post-training split is the load-bearing original claim and rests on the weakest data. If expert annotation volumes are much smaller than the InstructGPT recipe suggests, or environment costs much lower, the 8 on the exposure index rises. It would have to rise a long way to change the ordering.

On the frameworks

The cost stack, the leverage scale and the exposure index were assembled for this dashboard. They are ways of arranging what is known. No claim to novelty is made for any of them.

Pretraining buys hardware.
Post-training buys people.
Inference buys buildings and power.
